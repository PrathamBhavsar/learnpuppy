# Study Habits of Top Students

Back to [Huberman Learning Index](/huberman-learning/index.md) | [Brain Index](/index.md). Related: [Self-Testing Protocol](/huberman-learning/self-testing-protocol.md), [Circadian Optimization](/huberman-learning/circadian-optimization.md).

> Source: Andrew Huberman, *Optimal Protocols for Studying & Learning*. Based on a survey of ~700 medical students analyzing the learning habits associated with the most successful students.

## The Survey

A large study surveyed approximately 700 medical students (roughly equal male/female) and analyzed the habits associated with the best-performing students. Causality cannot be established from a survey (are they good students because they study this way, or do they study this way because they're good students?), but the habits are consistent across students and courses.

## Top 6 Habits

### 1. Schedule Study Time
- Literally schedule time to study — put it in the calendar
- Clears out other distractions
- Creates a psychological commitment to the learning bout

### 2. Study Alone
- The best-performing students study **alone**, not in groups
- Isolation removes social distraction
- Allows full focus on the material
- Group study can be effective, but solo study correlated most strongly with top performance

### 3. Eliminate Distractions
- Phone away and **off** — not just silenced, physically out of sight
- Tell friends and family you will be unreachable during study time
- Turn off Wi-Fi if not needed for studying
- Close unnecessary browser tabs

### 4. 3–4 Hours Per Day in 2–3 Sessions
- Not one long marathon session
- Broken into 2–3 focused sessions
- At least 5 days per week
- Consistency matters more than total hours on any given day

### 5. Teach Peers
- After studying alone, bring the material to other students and **teach them**
- This tests and develops mastery — you discover what you actually understand vs what you think you understand
- Counterintuitive: in competitive environments, teaching peers seems like giving away an advantage
- But the teaching student performs exceedingly well — the act of teaching consolidates their own knowledge

> "Watch one, do one, teach one."

This lab principle applies to any skill where safety is not at risk:
1. **Watch** someone else perform the skill
2. **Do** it yourself
3. **Teach** it to someone else

### 6. Aspirational Motivation
- The best students had a **long-term, abstract, aspirational** understanding of how their success would impact their life and family
- Not specific about exactly how — broad and motivational
- This "pulls them forward" through study efforts when desire wanes or fatigue increases
- Individual and highly personal — the specific aspiration doesn't matter, having one does

> "It bridges the two extremes of studying and learning — the nitty-gritty of how long they study, and the underlying psychological motivations."

## Entrainment: Regular Timing

- After 2–3 days of studying at the same time, the brain **entrains** to that schedule
- Focus and attention become easier simply by regularity
- Like sleep-wake cycles entraining to sunlight and social rhythms
- Keep the schedule even on weekends (especially near exams)
- Allow yourself the 2–3 day transition period to break in

## The Phone Rule

> "Walking out of class and getting immediately onto your phone inhibits your ability to remember the material."

After exposure to new material:
- **Don't** go on your phone
- **Do** test yourself on what you just learned — even a few seconds of mental retrieval

The window right after exposure is when self-testing is most effective. Phone use during this window enhances forgetting.

## Skill Levels

| Level | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Unskilled** | Limited understanding, cannot use information |
| **Skilled** | Know and can recognize and use information in basic/advanced ways |
| **Mastery** | Close to full depth of knowledge, can use it flexibly |
| **Virtuosity** | Such mastery that you can invite in uncertainty and spontaneity — surprising even yourself |

> "Most of us would be content to have skill and mastery of the things that we care about. Should we achieve virtuosity, then wonderful."
