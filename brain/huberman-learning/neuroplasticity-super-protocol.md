# The Neuroplasticity Super-Protocol (9 Steps)

Back to [Huberman Learning Index](/huberman-learning/index.md) | [Brain Index](/index.md). Related: [Neuroplasticity Fundamentals](/huberman-learning/neuroplasticity-fundamentals.md), [Self-Testing Protocol](/huberman-learning/self-testing-protocol.md).

> Source: Andrew Huberman, *Teach & Learn Better With A "Neuroplasticity Super Protocol"* — Huberman Lab Neural Network Newsletter. This version focuses on behavioral tools.

## Overview

The 9-step protocol combines everything neuroscience knows about triggering and completing neuroplasticity. You don't have to do all 9 every session — **steps 1, 2, and 9 are non-negotiable**.

```
1. Get Alert → 2. Get Focused → 3. Generate Repetitions → 4. Expect Errors
→ 5. Micro-Rest Intervals → 6. Random Reward → 7. 90-Min Limit
→ 8. NSDR After → 9. Quality Sleep
```

## Step 1: Get Alert

We must be alert to trigger neuroplasticity. Getting alert involves the release of **epinephrine** (adrenaline) in the brain and body.

**Tool:** 25–30 deep breaths (inhale through nose, exhale through mouth). Then exhale all air and hold breath with lungs empty for 15–60 seconds. Then inhale once and hold — but don't force it; start breathing normally when you feel the impulse.

Whether or not you use caffeine, try this **before** a learning bout.

## Step 2: Get Focused

Mental focus follows visual focus. To increase focus on the task ahead:

**Tool:** Stare at a point on a wall, screen, or object for 30–60 seconds before starting (blink as needed). The effort you feel is **top-down attentional engagement** — acetylcholine release and other mechanisms. Then move into the task.

Expect mental focus to flicker on and off, especially at the start of a learning bout. This is normal.

**Environment:** Phone off and out of the room. Browsers closed or limited to essential tabs only. Internet off if possible.

## Step 3: Generate Repetitions

Perform the maximum number of repetitions you safely can in a given learning bout.

- For some learning, "repetitions" are literal repeats (music scales, math problems)
- For others, it's repeating the same process (reading, analyzing)
- Work to repeat the process **slightly faster than is reflexive** — this keeps the mind from drifting and naturally maintains alertness

## Step 4: Expect & Embrace Errors

Provided they don't compromise safety, errors during learning are **terrific** because they increase activation of neural circuits that increase alertness.

> "If you perform something correctly, why should your brain take notice? When we make errors, it feels 'stressful,' but that is just an increase in attention that puts us in a much better place to perform and execute the next attempt."

**Optimal error rate: ~15%.** Computational modeling suggests this is the sweet spot for how difficult a task should be.

Don't worry about the exact number — keep doing repetitions and when you mess up, capitalize on it by doing another attempt while your forebrain is in that maximally attentive state.

## Step 5: Insert Micro-Rest Intervals (at Random)

A non-obvious way to increase repetitions and learn faster.

**Tool:** During learning, randomly pause for **10 seconds** and do nothing. During the pause, neurons in the hippocampus and cortex engage the same patterns of neural activity that occurred during the actual learning — but **10x faster**. You get 10x neural repetitions completed during the pause.

- Ratio: approximately **1 pause per every 2 minutes** of learning
- **Distributed at random** — not every 2 minutes on the minute
- These "gap effects" are similar to what happens in deep sleep

## Step 6: Use Random Intermittent Reward

The neural circuits that control rewards are closely tethered to the circuits that control motivation and the desire to pursue things, including learning.

**Rule:** Make rewards **random and intermittent**. This is what casinos do to keep people gambling. Predictable rewards lose their motivational impact quickly.

## Step 7: Limit Learning Sessions to 90 Minutes

- 90 minutes is about the longest period for maintaining intense focus and effort
- Shorter bouts are fine
- After ~90 minutes, take a break
- Space intense learning bouts **2–3+ hours apart**
- Most people can't do more than **270 minutes** (4.5 hours) of intense learning bouts per day

## Step 8: After a Learning Bout, Do NSDR

Within **1 hour** of completing a learning bout, do a short NSDR protocol:

- **Reveri** — zero-cost, research-tested self-hypnosis app
- **20-minute nap**
- **Yoga Nidra** — 10-minute or 30-minute scripts available

Two recent studies (in humans) show that shallow naps and/or NSDR can enhance the rate and depth of learning.

## Step 9: Get Quality & Sufficiently Long Deep Sleep

The actual rewiring of neural circuits that underlies learning occurs **during sleep and NSDR**. Think of the learning bout as the "trigger" — sleep and NSDR are when the actual learning (neural circuit rewiring) occurs.

- Goal: get sleep right at least 80% of the time
- It takes work to get there, but it is well worth it
- See Huberman Lab sleep protocols for details

## Non-Negotiables

Steps 1 (get alert), 2 (get focused), and 9 (quality sleep) are non-negotiable. The others enhance the process but are optional per session.
