# Neuroplasticity Fundamentals — How the Brain Learns

Back to [Huberman Learning Index](/huberman-learning/index.md) | [Brain Index](/index.md). Related: [Self-Testing Protocol](/huberman-learning/self-testing-protocol.md), [Neuroplasticity Super-Protocol](/huberman-learning/neuroplasticity-super-protocol.md).

> Source: Andrew Huberman, *Optimal Protocols for Studying & Learning* (Huberman Lab Podcast).

## The Core Reframe

> "Rather than think about studying to learn and retain information, I want you to think about studying to offset the natural process of forgetting that everybody experiences when they are exposed to new material of any kind."

Learning is not about absorbing information. It is about **inoculating against forgetting**. This reframe changes every study decision.

## Three Mechanisms of Neuroplasticity

| Mechanism | Description | Role in learning |
|-----------|-------------|------------------|
| **Strengthening** of synaptic connections | More robust communication between neurons | Primary mechanism of learning |
| **Weakening** of synaptic connections | Removal of connections | Also critical — motor skill improvement is largely removal of unnecessary connections |
| **Neurogenesis** (new neurons) | Addition of new neurons | Near-trivial contribution; only in olfactory bulb and dentate gyrus of hippocampus |

> "The improvement in motor coordination from birth to adulthood is largely the reflection of the **removal** of neural connections as opposed to the formation of new connections. However, the neural connections that remain become much more robust."

Neither strengthening nor weakening maps directly to "forming" or "removing" memories. Don't project value onto the mechanism — both are essential.

## The Two-Step Process

Neuroplasticity and learning is a **two-step process**:

### Step 1: Alert Focus (the trigger)
- You must be **alert** to trigger neuroplasticity
- You must be **focused** on the material
- Focus + alertness cues the nervous system that something is important and needs to change
- At the neurochemical level: **epinephrine** (adrenaline) release in brain and body
- The **strain** you feel when forcing yourself to focus is the cue — it means neuroplasticity is being triggered
- This is "active engagement" — not passive exposure

### Step 2: Sleep & Rest (the rewiring)
- The actual remodeling of neural connections — the strengthening and weakening — occurs **during sleep**, not during the learning bout
- Specifically during **rapid eye movement (REM) sleep**, which predominates in the latter half of the night
- The learning bout is the **trigger**; sleep is where the actual learning (neural rewiring) happens

> "You trigger the change, and in sleep, you get the change."

## Sleep: The Best Nootropic

- Sleep is "without question, the best nootropic" (smart drug)
- Your ability to be alert and focused is greater if you slept well the night before
- Some stress is good (cues alertness), but get sleep right at least 80% of the time

### The First-Night Effect
- Information learned on a given day is mostly consolidated during the **first night's sleep** after learning
- A poor first night does not mean information is lost forever, but the first night is the most important
- Pulling all-nighters or drinking caffeine late disrupts this consolidation

## NSDR (Non-Sleep Deep Rest)

- Also called Yoga Nidra
- 10–20 minute practice that restores mental and physical vigor
- Can be done: first thing in morning (if sleep-deprived), afternoon, or middle of night (to fall back asleep)
- **Enhances neuroplasticity** — the reordering of neural connections
- Recent studies show shallow naps and NSDR enhance the rate and depth of learning
- Should be done **within 1 hour** of completing a learning bout
- Options: Reveri app (self-hypnosis), 20-minute nap, Yoga Nidra script
- Huberman does a 10-minute NSDR daily

## Focus as a Trainable Skill

Focus is a **limited but renewable resource**:
- The longer you're awake, the more **adenosine** builds up → makes you sleepy, harder to focus
- Sleep pushes adenosine down → restores ability to focus

### Training focus
1. **Mindfulness meditation** (5–10 min/day) — sit/lie down, close eyes, focus on breath. Attention drifts → bring it back. Improves focus, memory, and recall. (Wendy Suzuki lab, NYU)
2. **Visual target exercise** — focus on a visual target 1–3 feet away, blink as needed, bring attention back when it drifts. 1–10 minutes.
3. Both train the circuits for focus and attention, which are themselves subject to neuroplasticity

### The strain is the signal
> "That strain that you feel in trying to learn, that agitation is great. That's a cue to your nervous system that it's going to need to change. That neuroplasticity needs to take place."

If learning feels effortless, the nervous system doesn't need to change — it already has the capability.
